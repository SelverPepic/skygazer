THE FIELD GUIDE

Learn the
language of clouds.

Cloud shape reflects how air moves and cools. It offers useful clues—but always read the wider atmosphere too.

01
↗

Air is lifted

Sun-warmed ground, fronts, hills or converging winds can push moist air upward.

02
◌

It cools and condenses

Rising air expands and cools. At its dew point, water droplets or ice crystals form on tiny particles.

03
☁

Clouds evolve

Stable air favours broad layers; buoyant, unstable air can build heaps and towers. Clouds fade as air warms or dries.

READ THE TREND

Pressure and temperature
are clues, not verdicts.

Pressure: falling pressure often accompanies an approaching low or front; rising pressure may follow a system or go with settling weather. The trend and local pattern matter more than one number.

Temperature: warming can reflect daytime sunshine or a warmer air mass; cooling can follow sunset, rain, or a cooler air mass. Cloud can reduce daytime heating and slow night-time cooling. Interpret a change alongside time of day and sky conditions.

Limits: station height, terrain, wind and humidity also matter. A grid estimate and one photograph cannot give a dependable local warning. Use your national weather service for safety decisions.
